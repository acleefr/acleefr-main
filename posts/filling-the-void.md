I've seen this play out for real, more than once: virtual agents and chat, inside omnichannel companies.

A standalone product doesn't usually die to a better competitor. It dies the day a bigger platform ships a rough version of the one thing that justified paying for it separately.

That's basically the story of standalone chat and virtual agent tools. For years they lived in the void: omnichannel platforms handled voice, email, and ticketing, but not a decent bot or a real-time chat widget. So a whole category of point solutions grew up around exactly that gap.

Then the platforms shipped it. Not a great virtual agent, not the best chat experience on the market, just good enough, already wired into the same contact history, the same agent desktop, the same billing. And the calculation flipped: staying inside one ecosystem, with one login, one support contact, beat a marginally better bot that lived outside it.

This is why "exact plus value" matters so much, and why it's so fragile. If your entire differentiation is one specific capability, you're not selling a product, you're renting a feature that hasn't been built yet by someone with more distribution than you.

The lesson for anyone building a feature inside a big platform: know exactly which smaller products live in the void you're about to fill. You're not just shipping a feature. You're deciding who survives.
